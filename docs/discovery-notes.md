# What Lena told us

Notes from the customer conversation with Lena, owner of Juniper Salon (23 of 50
turns). Quotes are hers, word for word. Each design choice links back to a
quote.

## Current process
- "Usually I notice the cancellation in Square, then Carla or I check the Google
  Sheet for people who might fit and text them from the salon phone. We have to
  keep checking replies and move down the list manually, and sometimes the
  appointment stays empty."
- The sheet holds "name, mobile number, requested service, preferred stylist, and
  general availability."
- A good fit must "match the service and time, plus any required stylist
  preference. If several fit, we go by who joined the waitlist earliest."
- "We often text several people because we're trying to fill the opening quickly."
- Square stays the real calendar: "We don't need this to update Square; staff
  handle the real calendar."

## Users
- Staff: "Carla and I handle it. Usually one of us is helping clients at the desk
  or dealing with the stylists, so the cancellation follow-up gets squeezed in
  between other things."
- Staff control: "We should be able to create the opening, see its status, and
  cancel it if the client changes their mind or the stylist becomes unavailable.
  We don't want to approve every step manually."
- Clients: waitlisted clients reached by text. "Some clients may opt out of texts,
  and that needs to be respected." "Clients shouldn't need to create accounts. It
  should feel simple and calm on a phone."

## What goes wrong
- Double booking: "Last month two clients expected the same Saturday haircut after
  both replied yes to a group text. Different staff members had answered them, so
  one client left angry."
- Dropped follow-ups: "We lose track of who was contacted and who declined, and
  sometimes nobody gets to the next person. Then the chair sits empty even though
  someone might have taken it."
- Late yeses: "We have to tell them it's gone, which is awkward."
- Silent failures: "Mostly an opening quietly stalling or a message not reaching
  someone. Replies can also be unclear, and we need to know when the process stops
  so we can handle it ourselves."

## How clients respond
- "They usually reply with a simple yes or no, but the timing varies. Some answer
  quickly and others don't respond before the opening is gone."
- No fixed wait today: "There isn't a fixed amount of time; we follow up when we can."
- Agreed rule: "one at a time in waitlist order feels fairer and avoids competing
  acceptances. For a same-day opening, give them 15 minutes, then move on if they
  haven't answered." Openings further out get longer, but she has no firm time.
- People who decline "generally stay on the waitlist for another opening."
- "There's no strict cutoff, though same-day openings are the hardest." She avoids
  unreasonable hours but has "no formal rule."

## What people need to see
- Staff: "who currently has the offer, who already declined or timed out, and who
  is still available. Also whether the opening was filled or cancelled."
- Clients: "the service, stylist, date, and time, and a clear way to accept or
  decline. After they reply, they should know whether they got the appointment or
  whether it was already taken."
- When filled: "The client and front desk should be notified when it's filled, and
  we need to tell other people the opening is no longer available."

## Business outcome
- Volume: "Around 8 to 12 cancellations inside 48 hours in a typical week."
- Cost: no exact figure, but an empty chair "affects both the salon and the stylist
  because they're paid on commission."
- Success: "We'd spend much less time chasing replies, and Carla would know when the
  process had stopped or failed. More cancellations would get filled, stylists
  would have fewer empty blocks, and clients would get a clear answer without
  competing claims."
- Top priority: "It needs to move reliably to the next person without
  double-booking. It also needs to show us clearly when an opening is filled,
  cancelled, or has stopped."

## Assumptions we made (she left these open)
| Open question | Prototype default | Why |
|---|---|---|
| How long to hold an offer that is not same-day | 60 minutes | She wants "longer" than 15 minutes but gave no number. It is one setting. |
| What happens to an unclear reply | The client is asked to reply YES or NO, the opening is flagged for staff, and the clock keeps running | She wants to know so staff can "handle it ourselves" without the opening stalling |
| What happens if a text cannot be delivered | Retried automatically, then that client is skipped and staff are told | Matches "a message not reaching someone" plus "we need to know" |
| Quiet hours | Not enforced; listed as a next step | She has "no formal rule" yet |
| A reply after someone's turn has passed | Polite "no longer available" text; they stay on the waitlist | No competing claims, and it removes the awkward manual reply |
